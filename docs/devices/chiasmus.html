Chiasmus

ky-AZ-mus / kaɪˈæzməs

A two-part structure whose second half inverts the order of the first: A-B / B-A. The crossing shape (named for the Greek letter chi, X) makes the sentence feel self-sealing — it ends by folding back through its own material, leaving nothing outside the pattern. Chiasmus of exact repeated words is antimetabole.

"Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country." — JFK

Don't confuse it with Antimetabole. Antimetabole reverses the *same words*; chiasmus may invert grammar or concepts without exact lexical repetition.
