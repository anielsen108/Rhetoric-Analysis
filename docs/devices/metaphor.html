Metaphor & Figuration

Metaphor

MET-uh-for / ˈmɛtəfɔːr

Asserting that one thing *is* another, transferring the second thing's properties wholesale without announcing the comparison. Because nothing signals the figure, the reader's mind performs the fusion itself — which is why metaphor persuades where argument can't: it installs the mapping before it can be inspected.

"Juliet is the sun." — Romeo and Juliet

Don't confuse it with Simile, Catachresis. Simile flags the comparison with like/as, keeping the two things separate. A metaphor sustained across a passage becomes extended metaphor or allegory; a strained, category-breaking one is catachresis.
