Narrative & Time

Analepsis

an-uh-LEP-sis / ˌænəˈlɛpsɪs

The flashback: narration steps out of the story's present to recount earlier events. It subordinates chronology to significance — the past arrives exactly when it will mean most — and reminds the reader that a narrator, not time, is in charge of order.

"Some years ago…" — Ishmael looking back

Don't confuse it with Prolepsis. Prolepsis is its forward-facing mirror, the flash-forward.
