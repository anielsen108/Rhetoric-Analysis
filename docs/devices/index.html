Repetition & Refrain

Repeating words and phrases — at openings, endings, edges, or in chains.

Anaphorauh-NAF-or-uh / əˈnæfərə

Beginning successive clauses, sentences, or lines with the same word or phrase.

71 excerpts Gradatio / Climaxgruh-DAH-tee-oh / ɡrəˈdɑːtioʊ

A ladder of linked clauses, each picking up the previous one's key word and raising the stakes, until the sequence peaks.

10 excerpts Epizeuxiseh-pih-ZOOK-sis / ˌɛpɪˈzjuːksɪs

Immediate repetition of a word with nothing between: words, words, words.

10 excerpts Polyptotonpah-lip-TOH-ton / pəˈlɪptətɒn

Repeating a word in different grammatical forms — please/pleasure, judge/judgment, dying/death.

9 excerpts Epistropheeh-PIS-truh-fee / ɪˈpɪstrəfi

Ending successive clauses or sentences with the same word or phrase.

4 excerpts Epanalepsiseh-puh-nuh-LEP-sis / ɛpənəˈlɛpsɪs

Repeating a clause's opening word or phrase at its own end, closing the loop: the sentence returns to where it began.

2 excerpts Anadiplosisan-uh-dih-PLOH-sis / ænədɪˈploʊsɪs

The last word of one clause becomes the first word of the next, linking clauses like a chain.

2 excerpts Diacopedy-ACK-oh-pee / daɪˈækəpiː

Repetition of a word or phrase with a short interruption between the repeats — the pattern of "Bond.

2 excerpts SymploceSIM-ploh-see / ˈsɪmploʊsiː

Anaphora and epistrophe combined: successive clauses share both their opening and their closing words, with variation trapped in the middle.

1 excerpt Conduplicatiokon-doo-plih-KAH-tee-oh / ˌkɒndjuːplɪˈkɑːtioʊ

Picking up a key word from one sentence or clause and redeploying it prominently in the next, dragging the concept forward for re-examination.

Balance & Opposition

Matched structures and clashing ideas — parallels, mirrors, contradictions.

ParallelismPAIR-uh-lel-iz-um / ˈpærəlɛlɪzəm

Expressing coordinate ideas in coordinate grammatical form — matching phrases with phrases, clauses with clauses.

41 excerpts Antithesisan-TIH-thuh-sis / ænˈtɪθəsɪs

Setting contrasting ideas in balanced, parallel grammar so the opposition itself becomes visible in the sentence's shape.

38 excerpts ParadoxPAIR-uh-doks / ˈpærədɒks

A statement that contradicts itself yet insists on being true — and, examined, yields a truth ordinary language couldn't hold.

25 excerpts Chiasmusky-AZ-mus / kaɪˈæzməs

A two-part structure whose second half inverts the order of the first: A-B / B-A.

13 excerpts Oxymoronok-see-MOR-on / ˌɒksɪˈmɔːrɒn

Two contradictory terms fused into one phrase — darkness visible, deafening silence.

12 excerpts Isocoloneye-soh-KOH-lon / ˌaɪsoʊˈkoʊlən

Successive phrases or clauses of matching length and identical grammatical shape — parallelism with the meter of a scale in balance.

7 excerpts Juxtapositionjuk-stuh-puh-ZISH-un

Placing two elements side by side without stated connection and letting the collision generate the meaning.

5 excerpts Antimetabolean-tee-muh-TAB-oh-lee / ˌæntimɛˈtæbəliː

Repeating the same words in reverse order — A-B / B-A with the identical vocabulary.

3 excerpts

Metaphor & Figuration

Saying one thing by another — transfer, substitution, embodiment.

MetaphorMET-uh-for / ˈmɛtəfɔːr

Asserting that one thing *is* another, transferring the second thing's properties wholesale without announcing the comparison.

57 excerpts Personificationper-sah-nih-fih-KAY-shun / pərˌsɒnɪfɪˈkeɪʃən

Granting human qualities, actions, or feelings to non-human things — nations conceive, winds sigh, justice sleeps.

49 excerpts SimileSIM-ih-lee / ˈsɪməliː

An explicit comparison marked by like or as.

21 excerpts Metonymymeh-TAH-nuh-mee / mɛˈtɒnəmi

Substituting a thing's name with something associated with it — the crown for the monarchy, the White House for the administration, the sword for war.

20 excerpts Synecdochesih-NEK-duh-kee / sɪˈnɛkdəkiː

Letting a part stand for the whole (hired hands for workers) or the whole for a part (the law for a police officer).

19 excerpts Allusionuh-LOO-zhun / əˈluːʒən

An unflagged reference to another text, event, or figure that imports its whole context into the new sentence.

12 excerpts Catachresiskat-uh-KREE-sis / ˌkætəˈkriːsɪs

A deliberately strained or 'wrong' metaphor — using a word where no literal word exists, or forcing a term outside its category until the semantic misfit itself is the effect.

5 excerpts Transferred Epithet / Hypallagehy-PAL-uh-jee / haɪˈpælədʒi

An adjective displaced from the noun it logically describes onto a neighboring noun — a condemned cell, a restless night.

4 excerpts Prosopopoeiaproh-soh-poh-PEE-uh / prəˌsoʊpəˈpiːə

Bringing an absent, dead, or non-human speaker onstage to talk — giving a voice, not just attributes.

3 excerpts Metalepsismet-uh-LEP-sis / ˌmɛtəˈlɛpsɪs

A figure built on another figure — reference at a remove, where the reader must traverse a chain of associations to arrive.

2 excerpts Pathetic Fallacypuh-THET-ik FAL-uh-see

Nature mirroring human emotion — storms for turmoil, spring for hope — as if the world felt what the character feels.

1 excerpt Symbol

A concrete thing that carries a field of abstract meaning beyond its literal presence — recurring, accumulating significance each time it returns.

1 excerpt Mythopoesismith-oh-poh-EE-sis

Myth-making: narration pitched at the register of origin stories, where characters become archetypes and events become cosmology.

Irony & Understatement

Meaning versus scale — overstating, understating, and saying the opposite.

LitotesLY-tuh-teez / ˈlaɪtətiːz (also lie-TOH-teez)

Affirming something by negating its opposite — not bad, no small feat, not unaware.

49 excerpts IronyEYE-ruh-nee / ˈaɪrəni

A gap between what is said and what is meant (verbal irony), between what a character knows and what the audience knows (dramatic irony), or between expectation and outcome (situational irony).

28 excerpts Hyperbolehy-PER-buh-lee / haɪˈpɜːrbəliː

Exaggeration beyond literal credibility, offered in the confidence that the reader will scale it back — and land exactly where the writer wanted.

26 excerpts EuphemismYOO-fuh-miz-um / ˈjuːfəmɪzəm

Substituting a mild or indirect expression for a harsh or taboo one — passed away, let go, collateral damage.

17 excerpts Meiosis / Understatementmy-OH-sis / maɪˈoʊsɪs

Deliberately representing something as smaller or less significant than it is — calling a wound 'a scratch.

9 excerpts Apophasisuh-POF-uh-sis / əˈpɒfəsɪs

Asserting something by declaring you will not mention it — 'I won't dwell on his record.

4 excerpts Periphrasispuh-RIF-ruh-sis / pəˈrɪfrəsɪs

Saying something in deliberately more words than needed — a roundabout route in place of the direct name.

3 excerpts BathosBAY-thos / ˈbeɪθɒs

A sudden fall from the elevated to the trivial — the sublime stubbing its toe.

2 excerpts

Sound & Diction

The texture of words themselves — echoes, coinages, deliberate wrongness.

Alliterationuh-lit-er-AY-shun / əˌlɪtəˈreɪʃən

Repetition of initial consonant sounds across nearby words.

7 excerpts ArchaismAR-kay-iz-um / ˈɑːrkeɪɪzəm

Deliberately outdated language — four score, thou, brought forth — imported for its patina.

6 excerpts Neologismnee-OL-uh-jiz-um / niˈɒlədʒɪzəm

A coined word — invented outright or fused from existing parts — filling a gap the lexicon didn't know it had.

5 excerpts Onomatopoeiaon-uh-mat-uh-PEE-uh / ˌɒnəˌmætəˈpiːə

Words whose sound imitates their sense — buzz, crack, murmur.

2 excerpts TmesisTMEE-sis / təˈmiːsɪs

Splitting a word or fixed phrase and wedging another word inside — abso-bloody-lutely.

2 excerpts SolecismSOL-uh-siz-um / ˈsɒləsɪzəm

A deliberate breach of grammar or usage deployed for effect — dialect, double negatives, non-standard agreement.

1 excerpt EpithetEP-ih-thet / ˈɛpɪθɛt

A characterizing adjective or phrase attached to a name or thing — wine-dark sea, swift-footed Achilles.

1 excerpt AssonanceASS-uh-nunce / ˈæsənəns

Repetition of vowel sounds within nearby words — the deep green sea.

Syntax & Word Order

The architecture of the sentence — inversion, suspension, accumulation.

Passive Voice

The grammatical subject receives the action rather than performing it — mistakes were made.

41 excerpts Parataxispair-uh-TAK-sis / ˌpærəˈtæksɪs

Placing clauses side by side without subordination — no because, although, or which to rank one thought under another.

37 excerpts Apposition / Appositiveap-uh-ZISH-un / ˌæpəˈzɪʃən

Placing a noun phrase beside another to rename or re-describe it — 'my brother, the doctor.

36 excerpts Enumeration / Accumulatioee-noo-mer-AH-tee-oh

Amassing details, examples, or charges in a deliberate pile — the catalogue as argument.

15 excerpts Parenthesispuh-REN-thuh-sis / pəˈrɛnθəsɪs

An aside inserted mid-sentence — set off by dashes, brackets, or commas — that interrupts the syntax without joining it.

14 excerpts ZeugmaZOOG-muh / ˈzuːɡmə

One verb (or governing word) yoked to two or more objects that require it in different senses — often one literal, one figurative.

13 excerpts Hyperbatonhy-PER-buh-ton / haɪˈpɜːrbətɒn

Wrenching normal word order — object before subject, adjective after noun, verb delayed — so that the displaced word lands with unnatural emphasis.

8 excerpts Periodic Sentencepeer-ee-OD-ik

A sentence that withholds its main clause (or essential verb) until the end, stacking subordinate material first.

6 excerpts Cleft Sentence

Splitting a simple sentence into two clauses to spotlight one element — 'It was Ishmael who survived' instead of 'Ishmael survived.

5 excerpts Cumulative (Loose) SentenceKYOOM-yuh-luh-tiv

Main clause first, then modifiers accrete after it — participles, absolutes, appositives — each adding focus like camera pulls after establishing the shot.

4 excerpts Anastropheuh-NAS-truh-fee / əˈnæstrəfi

Inversion of the usual order of a pair of words — adjective after noun, preposition after object.

3 excerpts Hendiadyshen-DY-uh-dis / hɛnˈdaɪədɪs

Expressing one complex idea as two nouns joined by 'and' instead of a noun with modifier — sound and fury rather than furious sound.

2 excerpts PleonasmPLEE-oh-naz-um / ˈpliːənæzəm

Redundancy of words — using more than the grammar strictly needs: I saw it with my own eyes.

2 excerpts

Omission & Flow

What gets left out or multiplied — connectives, endings, line breaks.

Asyndetonuh-SIN-duh-ton / əˈsɪndətɒn

Omitting the conjunctions a list or clause-series would normally carry ("I came, I saw, I conquered" rather than "and I conquered").

42 excerpts Polysyndetonpah-lee-SIN-duh-ton / ˌpɒlɪˈsɪndətɒn

Multiplying conjunctions beyond grammatical need — and… and… and — so every item in a series gets equal, deliberate weight.

29 excerpts Enjambmenten-JAM-ment / ɪnˈdʒæmmənt

A verse line that ends before its sentence does, forcing the reader over the line-break to complete the thought.

12 excerpts Ellipsisee-LIP-sis / ɪˈlɪpsɪs

Omitting words that grammar requires but context supplies — 'I took the high road; she, the low.

4 excerpts Aposiopesisap-oh-sy-oh-PEE-sis / ˌæpəsaɪəˈpiːsɪs

Breaking off mid-sentence and leaving it unfinished — the dash into silence.

4 excerpts Brachylogiabrak-ih-LOH-jee-uh / ˌbrækɪˈloʊdʒiə

Extreme compression: single words strung without conjunctions — a telegraphic staccato.

1 excerpt

Address & Argument

The speaker turning to face someone — questions, appeals, pivots, maxims.

Apostropheuh-POS-truh-fee / əˈpɒstrəfi

Turning from the actual audience to address someone absent, dead, or something non-human — O wild West Wind.

8 excerpts Rhetorical Question(erotema: air-oh-TEE-muh)

A question posed for effect, whose answer is presumed or performed rather than sought.

7 excerpts Correctio / Epanorthosiskor-REK-tee-oh / eh-pan-or-THOH-sis

Saying something and immediately amending it — 'a hundred, no, a thousand times.

4 excerpts Enargeia / Vivid Specificityen-AR-gay-uh / ɛnɑːrˈɡeɪə

Description so concrete and sensory that the audience seems to witness rather than read — the classical virtue of 'bringing before the eyes.

4 excerpts Invocationin-voh-KAY-shun

A formal appeal to a muse, deity, or power to inspire and authorize the work — the epic's opening gesture.

2 excerpts AphorismAF-or-iz-um / ˈæfərɪzəm

A compact, freestanding statement of general truth, polished until it feels inevitable.

1 excerpt Parrhesiapa-RAY-zhuh / pəˈreɪziə

Bold, risky frankness — saying the dangerous thing plainly, and often flagging the risk ('I'll speak freely').

1 excerpt Metabasismeh-TAB-uh-sis / mɛˈtæbəsɪs

A brief transitional summary: what has been said, what comes next.

1 excerpt Amplificatioam-plih-fih-KAH-tee-oh

Expanding and intensifying a point through repetition, accumulation, and escalation — returning to the same idea with greater force each pass.

1 excerpt EnthymemeEN-thih-meem / ˈɛnθɪmiːm

A syllogism with a premise left silent, trusting the audience to supply it.

glossary only Aporiauh-POR-ee-uh / əˈpɔːriə

Performed doubt or perplexity — the speaker pauses to wonder aloud what to say, how to proceed, whether the thing can be said at all.

Narrative & Time

How telling handles time and mind — flashback, foreshadow, inner voice.

Free Indirect Discourse(FID)

Third-person narration that absorbs a character's voice, idiom, and judgments without quotation marks or 'she thought' tags.

5 excerpts Prolepsisproh-LEP-sis / proʊˈlɛpsɪs

Anticipation: treating a future event as already accomplished, or answering an objection before it is raised.

4 excerpts In Medias Resin MAY-dee-ahs RACE / ɪn ˈmɛdiəs ˈreɪs

Beginning in the middle of the action, with context withheld and backstory deferred.

2 excerpts Analepsisan-uh-LEP-sis / ˌænəˈlɛpsɪs

The flashback: narration steps out of the story's present to recount earlier events.

1 excerpt Foreshadowing

Planting details that quietly predict later events, legible fully only in retrospect.

1 excerpt Stream of Consciousness

Prose that imitates the unedited flow of a mind — associative leaps, unfinished syntax, sensation and memory interleaved without hierarchy.

1 excerpt Stichomythiastik-oh-MITH-ee-uh / ˌstɪkəˈmɪθiə

Dialogue exchanged in rapid, alternating single lines, each speaker capping or reversing the other's words.
