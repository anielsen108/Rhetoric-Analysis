Repetition & Refrain

Repeating words and phrases — at openings, endings, edges, or in chains.

Anaphorauh-NAF-or-uh / əˈnæfərə

Starting several clauses, sentences, or lines in a row with the same word or phrase.

71 excerpts Gradatio / Climaxgruh-DAH-tee-oh / ɡrəˈdɑːtioʊ

A chain of linked clauses, each picking up the last one's key word and raising the stakes until the sequence peaks.

10 excerpts Epizeuxiseh-pih-ZOOK-sis / ˌɛpɪˈzjuːksɪs

Repeating a word immediately, with nothing in between: 'words, words, words.

10 excerpts Polyptotonpah-lip-TOH-ton / pəˈlɪptətɒn

Repeating a word in different forms — please/pleasure, judge/judgment.

9 excerpts Epistropheeh-PIS-truh-fee / ɪˈpɪstrəfi

Ending several clauses or sentences in a row with the same word or phrase.

4 excerpts Epanalepsiseh-puh-nuh-LEP-sis / ɛpənəˈlɛpsɪs

Starting and ending a clause with the same word or phrase, so the sentence circles back to where it began.

2 excerpts Anadiplosisan-uh-dih-PLOH-sis / ænədɪˈploʊsɪs

The last word of one clause becomes the first word of the next, linking them like a chain.

2 excerpts Diacopedy-ACK-oh-pee / daɪˈækəpiː

Repeating a word or phrase with a short interruption in between — 'Bond.

2 excerpts SymploceSIM-ploh-see / ˈsɪmploʊsiː

Anaphora and epistrophe at once: several clauses in a row share both their opening and their closing words, with only the middle changing.

1 excerpt Conduplicatiokon-doo-plih-KAH-tee-oh / ˌkɒndjuːplɪˈkɑːtioʊ

Taking a key word from one sentence and placing it prominently in the next.

Balance & Opposition

Matched structures and clashing ideas — parallels, mirrors, contradictions.

ParallelismPAIR-uh-lel-iz-um / ˈpærəlɛlɪzəm

Putting matching ideas in matching grammatical form — phrases with phrases, clauses with clauses.

41 excerpts Antithesisan-TIH-thuh-sis / ænˈtɪθəsɪs

Contrasting ideas placed in balanced, matching grammar, so the contrast shows in the sentence's shape.

38 excerpts ParadoxPAIR-uh-doks / ˈpærədɒks

A statement that contradicts itself but turns out to be true in a way ordinary wording couldn't reach.

25 excerpts Chiasmusky-AZ-mus / kaɪˈæzməs

A two-part structure where the second half reverses the order of the first: A-B, then B-A.

13 excerpts Oxymoronok-see-MOR-on / ˌɒksɪˈmɔːrɒn

Two contradictory words fused into one phrase — deafening silence, living death.

12 excerpts Isocoloneye-soh-KOH-lon / ˌaɪsoʊˈkoʊlən

Phrases or clauses in a row with the same shape and nearly the same length.

7 excerpts Juxtapositionjuk-stuh-puh-ZISH-un

Two things placed side by side with no stated connection, letting the pairing make the meaning.

5 excerpts Antimetabolean-tee-muh-TAB-oh-lee / ˌæntimɛˈtæbəliː

Repeating the same words in reverse order: A-B, then B-A.

3 excerpts

Metaphor & Figuration

Saying one thing through another.

MetaphorMET-uh-for / ˈmɛtəfɔːr

Saying one thing is another, without announcing the comparison.

57 excerpts Personificationper-sah-nih-fih-KAY-shun / pərˌsɒnɪfɪˈkeɪʃən

Giving human qualities, actions, or feelings to non-human things — nations conceive, winds sigh.

49 excerpts SimileSIM-ih-lee / ˈsɪməliː

A comparison marked with like or as.

21 excerpts Metonymymeh-TAH-nuh-mee / mɛˈtɒnəmi

Replacing a thing's name with something associated with it — the crown for the monarchy, the White House for the administration.

20 excerpts Synecdochesih-NEK-duh-kee / sɪˈnɛkdəkiː

A part standing for the whole (hired hands for workers) or the whole for a part (the law for one officer).

19 excerpts Allusionuh-LOO-zhun / əˈluːʒən

An unmarked reference to another text, event, or figure, bringing its whole context along.

12 excerpts Catachresiskat-uh-KREE-sis / ˌkætəˈkriːsɪs

A deliberately strained or 'wrong' metaphor — a word forced outside its normal use until the misfit itself is the effect.

5 excerpts Transferred Epithet / Hypallagehy-PAL-uh-jee / haɪˈpælədʒi

An adjective moved from the noun it logically belongs to onto a neighbor — a sleepless night, a condemned cell.

4 excerpts Prosopopoeiaproh-soh-poh-PEE-uh / prəˌsoʊpəˈpiːə

Giving an absent, dead, or non-human speaker actual lines to say — a voice, not just human qualities.

3 excerpts Metalepsismet-uh-LEP-sis / ˌmɛtəˈlɛpsɪs

A figure built on another figure — a reference the reader reaches only through a chain of associations.

2 excerpts Pathetic Fallacypuh-THET-ik FAL-uh-see

Nature mirroring human emotion — storms for turmoil, spring for hope — as if the world felt what the character feels.

1 excerpt Symbol

A concrete thing that carries meanings beyond itself, gathering more each time it returns.

1 excerpt Mythopoesismith-oh-poh-EE-sis

Writing pitched at the level of origin stories, where characters become archetypes and events explain the world, not just the plot.

Irony & Understatement

Meaning versus scale — overstating, understating, and saying the opposite.

LitotesLY-tuh-teez / ˈlaɪtətiːz (also lie-TOH-teez)

Stating something by denying its opposite — not bad, no small feat.

49 excerpts IronyEYE-ruh-nee / ˈaɪrəni

A gap between what is said and what is meant, between what a character knows and what the audience knows, or between expectation and outcome.

28 excerpts Hyperbolehy-PER-buh-lee / haɪˈpɜːrbəliː

Exaggeration past literal belief, trusting the reader to scale it back.

26 excerpts EuphemismYOO-fuh-miz-um / ˈjuːfəmɪzəm

A mild or indirect phrase in place of a harsh or taboo one — passed away, let go, collateral damage.

17 excerpts Meiosis / Understatementmy-OH-sis / maɪˈoʊsɪs

Deliberately calling something smaller than it is — a wound is 'a scratch.

9 excerpts Apophasisuh-POF-uh-sis / əˈpɒfəsɪs

Claiming you won't mention something while mentioning it: 'I won't dwell on his record.

4 excerpts Periphrasispuh-RIF-ruh-sis / pəˈrɪfrəsɪs

Saying something in more words than needed — a roundabout route instead of the direct name.

3 excerpts BathosBAY-thos / ˈbeɪθɒs

A sudden drop from the lofty to the trivial.

2 excerpts

Sound & Diction

The texture of words themselves — echoes, coinages, deliberate wrongness.

Alliterationuh-lit-er-AY-shun / əˌlɪtəˈreɪʃən

Repeating the first consonant sound across nearby words.

7 excerpts ArchaismAR-kay-iz-um / ˈɑːrkeɪɪzəm

Deliberately old-fashioned language — four score, thou — used for its aura.

6 excerpts Neologismnee-OL-uh-jiz-um / niˈɒlədʒɪzəm

An invented word, coined outright or fused from existing parts.

5 excerpts Onomatopoeiaon-uh-mat-uh-PEE-uh / ˌɒnəˌmætəˈpiːə

Words that sound like what they mean — buzz, crack, murmur.

2 excerpts TmesisTMEE-sis / təˈmiːsɪs

Splitting a word or set phrase and wedging another word inside: abso-bloody-lutely.

2 excerpts SolecismSOL-uh-siz-um / ˈsɒləsɪzəm

A deliberate breach of standard grammar — dialect, double negatives, non-standard forms — used for effect.

1 excerpt EpithetEP-ih-thet / ˈɛpɪθɛt

A characterizing adjective or phrase attached to a name — wine-dark sea, swift-footed Achilles.

1 excerpt AssonanceASS-uh-nunce / ˈæsənəns

Repeating vowel sounds in nearby words — the deep green sea.

Syntax & Word Order

How sentences are built — word order, delay, pile-up.

Passive Voice

The subject receives the action instead of doing it: 'mistakes were made.

41 excerpts Parataxispair-uh-TAK-sis / ˌpærəˈtæksɪs

Clauses set side by side with no subordinating words — no because, although, or which.

37 excerpts Apposition / Appositiveap-uh-ZISH-un / ˌæpəˈzɪʃən

A noun phrase placed beside another to rename it: 'my brother, the doctor.

36 excerpts Enumeration / Accumulatioee-noo-mer-AH-tee-oh

Piling up details, examples, or charges into a deliberate list.

15 excerpts Parenthesispuh-REN-thuh-sis / pəˈrɛnθəsɪs

An aside dropped into the middle of a sentence — set off by dashes, brackets, or commas — that interrupts without joining the grammar.

14 excerpts ZeugmaZOOG-muh / ˈzuːɡmə

One verb governing two or more objects in different senses — often one literal, one figurative: 'She arrived in a taxi and a flood of tears.

13 excerpts Hyperbatonhy-PER-buh-ton / haɪˈpɜːrbətɒn

Breaking normal word order — object first, verb delayed, adjective after noun — so the displaced word lands with extra force.

8 excerpts Periodic Sentencepeer-ee-OD-ik

A sentence that saves its main clause for the end, stacking qualifying material first.

6 excerpts Cleft Sentence

Splitting a simple sentence in two to spotlight one part: 'It was Ishmael who survived' instead of 'Ishmael survived.

5 excerpts Cumulative (Loose) SentenceKYOOM-yuh-luh-tiv

Main clause first, then modifiers added after it, each one sharpening the picture.

4 excerpts Anastropheuh-NAS-truh-fee / əˈnæstrəfi

Reversing the usual order of a word pair — adjective after noun, preposition after object: 'the forest primeval.

3 excerpts Hendiadyshen-DY-uh-dis / hɛnˈdaɪədɪs

One complex idea expressed as two nouns joined by 'and' — sound and fury instead of furious sound.

2 excerpts PleonasmPLEE-oh-naz-um / ˈpliːənæzəm

Using more words than the grammar needs: 'I saw it with my own eyes.

2 excerpts

Omission & Flow

What gets left out or multiplied — connectives, endings, line breaks.

Asyndetonuh-SIN-duh-ton / əˈsɪndətɒn

Leaving out the connecting words a list would normally have: 'I came, I saw, I conquered' with no 'and.

42 excerpts Polysyndetonpah-lee-SIN-duh-ton / ˌpɒlɪˈsɪndətɒn

Using more connecting words than needed — and… and… and.

29 excerpts Enjambmenten-JAM-ment / ɪnˈdʒæmmənt

A verse line that ends before its sentence does, pushing the reader across the break.

12 excerpts Ellipsisee-LIP-sis / ɪˈlɪpsɪs

Leaving out words the grammar needs but the context supplies: 'I took the high road; she, the low.

4 excerpts Aposiopesisap-oh-sy-oh-PEE-sis / ˌæpəsaɪəˈpiːsɪs

Breaking off mid-sentence and leaving it unfinished — a dash into silence.

4 excerpts Brachylogiabrak-ih-LOH-jee-uh / ˌbrækɪˈloʊdʒiə

Extreme compression: single words strung together without connectives.

Address & Argument

The speaker turning to face someone — questions, appeals, pivots, maxims.

Apostropheuh-POS-truh-fee / əˈpɒstrəfi

Turning from the real audience to address someone absent, dead, or something non-human: 'O wild West Wind.

8 excerpts Rhetorical Question(erotema: air-oh-TEE-muh)

A question asked for effect, with the answer assumed rather than sought.

7 excerpts Correctio / Epanorthosiskor-REK-tee-oh / eh-pan-or-THOH-sis

Saying something and immediately revising it: 'a hundred — no, a thousand times.

4 excerpts Enargeia / Vivid Specificityen-AR-gay-uh / ɛnɑːrˈɡeɪə

Description so concrete and sensory that the audience seems to see it rather than read it.

4 excerpts Invocationin-voh-KAY-shun

A formal appeal to a muse, god, or power to inspire the work — the classic epic opening.

2 excerpts AphorismAF-or-iz-um / ˈæfərɪzəm

A short, freestanding statement of general truth, polished until it sounds inevitable.

1 excerpt Parrhesiapa-RAY-zhuh / pəˈreɪziə

Bold, risky frankness — saying the dangerous thing plainly, often flagging the risk first: 'I'll speak freely.

1 excerpt Metabasismeh-TAB-uh-sis / mɛˈtæbəsɪs

A brief transition that sums up what has been said and previews what comes next: 'So much for the causes; now the consequences.

1 excerpt Amplificatioam-plih-fih-KAH-tee-oh

Returning to the same point with more force each time — repeating, stacking, escalating until the idea fills the frame.

1 excerpt EnthymemeEN-thih-meem / ˈɛnθɪmiːm

An argument with one premise left unstated, trusting the audience to supply it.

glossary only Aporiauh-POR-ee-uh / əˈpɔːriə

Performed doubt: the speaker wonders aloud what to say, or whether it can be said at all.

Narrative & Time

How telling handles time and mind — flashback, foreshadow, inner voice.

Free Indirect Discourse(FID)

Third-person narration that takes on a character's voice and judgments without quotation marks or 'she thought' tags.

5 excerpts Prolepsisproh-LEP-sis / proʊˈlɛpsɪs

Anticipation: treating a future event as already settled, or answering an objection before it is raised.

4 excerpts In Medias Resin MAY-dee-ahs RACE / ɪn ˈmɛdiəs ˈreɪs

Starting in the middle of the action, with backstory held for later.

2 excerpts Analepsisan-uh-LEP-sis / ˌænəˈlɛpsɪs

The flashback: the narration steps out of the present to tell earlier events.

1 excerpt Foreshadowing

Details planted early that quietly predict later events, fully readable only in hindsight.

1 excerpt Stream of Consciousness

Prose imitating the raw flow of a mind — jumps of association, unfinished syntax, memory and sensation mixed together.

1 excerpt Stichomythiastik-oh-MITH-ee-uh / ˌstɪkəˈmɪθiə

Dialogue traded in quick single lines, each speaker capping or reversing the other's words.

Every verb that could carry responsibility has been converted into a noun and buried; what remains is syntax with no one inside it. 122 2025 A Neighbourhood Bistro — Autumn Menu (a composed specimen) The Bistro Menu Hyperbole applied uniformly until it cancels itself, "literally" deployed to mean its own opposite, and epithets stacked where evidence should be.
