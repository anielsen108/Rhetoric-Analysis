Parrhesia

pa-RAY-zhuh / pəˈreɪziə

Bold, risky frankness — saying the dangerous thing plainly, and often flagging the risk ('I'll speak freely'). The announced candor is itself rhetorical: it buys credibility for everything that follows, since a speaker willing to offend is presumed unwilling to lie.

Kent to Lear: "be Kent unmannerly, / When Lear is mad… What wouldst thou do, old man?"

Don't confuse it with Apophasis. Apophasis pretends *not* to say the thing; parrhesia says it and points at its own daring.
