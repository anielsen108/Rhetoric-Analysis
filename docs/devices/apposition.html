Apposition / Appositive

ap-uh-ZISH-un / ˌæpəˈzɪʃən

Placing a noun phrase beside another to rename or re-describe it — 'my brother, the doctor.' Each appositive is a second take: the writer looks at the same thing twice and hands the reader both exposures. Stacked appositives create incantatory redefinition, each one correcting the aim.

"124 was spiteful. Full of a baby's venom." — Morrison (appositive fragment)

Don't confuse it with Parenthesis. Parenthesis interrupts without renaming; apposition always re-identifies the neighboring noun.
