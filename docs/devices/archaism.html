Sound & Diction

Archaism

AR-kay-iz-um / ˈɑːrkeɪɪzəm

Deliberately outdated language — four score, thou, brought forth — imported for its patina. Old words carry their era's authority with them: scripture, epic, law. The reader hears ancestry, and the sentence claims a place in the long line it echoes.

"Four score and seven years ago…" — Lincoln

Don't confuse it with Allusion. Allusion points to a specific prior text; archaism borrows a period's diction wholesale without citing anyone.
