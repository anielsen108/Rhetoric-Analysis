Syntax & Word Order

Hyperbaton

hy-PER-buh-ton / haɪˈpɜːrbətɒn

Wrenching normal word order — object before subject, adjective after noun, verb delayed — so that the displaced word lands with unnatural emphasis. English word order is rigid, which makes every violation loud: the reader feels grammar itself strained by the pressure of the thought.

"Some rise by sin, and some by virtue fall." — Measure for Measure

Don't confuse it with Anastrophe. Anastrophe is the mild, single-pair inversion; hyperbaton covers more violent or extended derangements.
