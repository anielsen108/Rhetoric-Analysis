Sound & Diction

Alliteration

uh-lit-er-AY-shun / əˌlɪtəˈreɪʃən

Repetition of initial consonant sounds across nearby words. It binds words into a perceptual unit before their meanings connect — the ear declares them related and the mind scrambles to agree. The oldest structuring device in English verse, and still the fastest way to make a phrase memorable.

"would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world" — Melville's w's

Don't confuse it with Assonance. Assonance repeats vowel sounds; consonance repeats consonants anywhere in the word, not just initially.
