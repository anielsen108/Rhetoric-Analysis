Syntax & Word Order

Periodic Sentence

peer-ee-OD-ik

A sentence that withholds its main clause (or essential verb) until the end, stacking subordinate material first. The reader is held in suspension, carrying every qualifier forward, until the close snaps the whole structure into meaning at once. It is syntax as delayed gratification — and a display of authorial control.

"Of Man's first disobedience, and the fruit / Of that forbidden tree… Sing, Heav'nly Muse." — Milton

Don't confuse it with. The loose/cumulative sentence is its opposite: main clause first, modifiers trailing after.
