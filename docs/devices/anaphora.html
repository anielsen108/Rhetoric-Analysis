Repetition & Refrain

Anaphora

uh-NAF-or-uh / əˈnæfərə

Beginning successive clauses, sentences, or lines with the same word or phrase. The repetition builds a drumbeat: each return to the anchor phrase raises the emotional pressure while the changing endings carry the argument forward. It is the signature device of oratory because an audience can feel the structure without seeing it.

"We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds, we shall fight in the fields…" — Churchill

Don't confuse it with Epistrophe, Symploce. Epistrophe repeats at clause *ends*; symploce does both at once.
