Prosopopoeia

proh-soh-poh-PEE-uh / prəˌsoʊpəˈpiːə

Bringing an absent, dead, or non-human speaker onstage to talk — giving a voice, not just attributes. Where personification says the storm raged, prosopopoeia lets the storm (or the ancestors, or the country) deliver lines. The borrowed voice carries authority the author could not claim directly.

Cicero making the state of Rome herself rebuke Catiline.

Don't confuse it with Personification. Personification stops at attributing human qualities; prosopopoeia hands over the microphone.
