Syntax & Word Order

Cleft Sentence

Splitting a simple sentence into two clauses to spotlight one element — 'It was Ishmael who survived' instead of 'Ishmael survived.' The cleft manufactures focus grammatically: the it-clause points, the relative clause carries the rest. English's main tool for stressing a chosen word without italics.

"It was the best butter." → "It was a bright cold day in April…" — Orwell's it-frame

Don't confuse it with. A pseudo-cleft uses 'what' ('What he wanted was peace'); both are focus machines, differing in which slot gets the spotlight.
