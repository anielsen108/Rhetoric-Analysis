Enthymeme

EN-thih-meem / ˈɛnθɪmiːm

A syllogism with a premise left silent, trusting the audience to supply it. The suppressed premise is the persuasive payload: what the audience provides themselves, they do not audit. Most real-world argument runs on enthymemes; finding the missing premise is where critique begins.

"He's a politician, so check your wallet." (Suppressed: all politicians are venal.)

Don't confuse it with. A full syllogism states both premises; an enthymeme's power is exactly the one it doesn't state.
