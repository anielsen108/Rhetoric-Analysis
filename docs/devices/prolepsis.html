Prolepsis

proh-LEP-sis / proʊˈlɛpsɪs

Anticipation: treating a future event as already accomplished, or answering an objection before it is raised. In narrative it is the flash-forward; in argument, the pre-emptive strike. Either way the audience's future is colonized in advance — resistance is narrated before it can occur.

"Many years later, as he faced the firing squad, Colonel Aureliano Buendía was to remember…" — García Márquez

Don't confuse it with Analepsis. Analepsis looks backward (flashback); prolepsis looks forward. In rhetoric, procatalepsis is the objection-answering subtype.
