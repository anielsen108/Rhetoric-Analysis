Apostrophe

uh-POS-truh-fee / əˈpɒstrəfi

Turning from the actual audience to address someone absent, dead, or something non-human — O wild West Wind. The swerve of address dramatizes overflow: feeling too great for the real audience gets aimed at the cosmos. It also quietly recasts the reader as eavesdropper on a private communion.

"O Romeo, Romeo, wherefore art thou Romeo?"

Don't confuse it with Invocation. Direct address speaks to the present audience ('Reader, I married him' sits between the two); invocation is apostrophe petitioning for aid.
