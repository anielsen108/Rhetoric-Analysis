Paradox

PAIR-uh-doks / ˈpærədɒks

A statement that contradicts itself yet insists on being true — and, examined, yields a truth ordinary language couldn't hold. Paradox forces the reader to abandon the frame in which the contradiction is impossible; it is the trope of mystics, lovers, and dialecticians.

"The child is father of the man." — Wordsworth

Don't confuse it with Antithesis, Oxymoron. Oxymoron is paradox compressed to a two-word collision ('living death'); antithesis balances opposites without asserting both are true.
