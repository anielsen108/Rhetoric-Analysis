Repetition & Refrain

Epistrophe

eh-PIS-truh-fee / ɪˈpɪstrəfi

Ending successive clauses or sentences with the same word or phrase. Where anaphora launches each clause from the same spot, epistrophe makes every clause land on the same spot, so the repeated word accumulates finality — each sentence hammers the same nail deeper.

"…government of the people, by the people, for the people" — Lincoln

Don't confuse it with Anaphora, Epanalepsis. Anaphora repeats at beginnings; epanalepsis repeats a clause's opening word at its own end.
