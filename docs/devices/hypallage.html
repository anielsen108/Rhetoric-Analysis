Transferred Epithet / Hypallage

hy-PAL-uh-jee / haɪˈpælədʒi

An adjective displaced from the noun it logically describes onto a neighboring noun — a condemned cell, a restless night. The grammar misassigns the feeling, and the misassignment is expressive: emotion has leaked out of the person and soaked into the furniture of the scene.

"Once more the storm is howling, and half hid / Under this cradle-hood and coverlid / My child sleeps on" → "murderous innocence of the sea" — Yeats

Don't confuse it with Epithet. A plain epithet sits on its rightful noun; hypallage is the deliberate misdelivery.
