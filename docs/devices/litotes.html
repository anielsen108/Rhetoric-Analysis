Irony & Understatement

Litotes

LY-tuh-teez / ˈlaɪtətiːz (also lie-TOH-teez)

Affirming something by negating its opposite — not bad, no small feat, not unaware. The double processing (reader must flip the negation) slows comprehension just enough to make the understatement felt as a choice: the speaker could have praised directly and pointedly didn't. It projects modesty, irony, or cool control.

"having little or no money in my purse" — Melville

Don't confuse it with. Meiosis is plain understatement without the negation mechanism; litotes specifically runs on negatives.
