Narrative & Time

Free Indirect Discourse

(FID)

Third-person narration that absorbs a character's voice, idiom, and judgments without quotation marks or 'she thought' tags. The narrator's grammar carries the character's mind, so inner life arrives unmediated — and irony becomes possible in the gap between the borrowed voice and the narrating intelligence. The great engine of the modern novel, from Austen to Woolf.

"What a lark! What a plunge!" — Mrs. Dalloway's mind in the narrator's sentence

Don't confuse it with. Direct discourse quotes; indirect discourse reports ('she thought that…'); FID deletes the tag and lets the voices blend.
