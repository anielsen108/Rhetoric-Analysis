Juxtaposition

juk-stuh-puh-ZISH-un

Placing two elements side by side without stated connection and letting the collision generate the meaning. The writer supplies proximity; the reader supplies the relation — irony, causation, contrast — and owns the inference. Montage in prose.

"The apparition of these faces in the crowd; / Petals on a wet, black bough." — Pound

Don't confuse it with Antithesis. Antithesis states its contrast in balanced grammar; juxtaposition just sets the two things down and walks away.
