Polysyndeton

pah-lee-SIN-duh-ton / ˌpɒlɪˈsɪndətɒn

Multiplying conjunctions beyond grammatical need — and… and… and — so every item in a series gets equal, deliberate weight. The effect can be biblical accumulation, a child's breathless narration, or exhaustion, depending on what is being stacked; the ands flatten hierarchy and make sequence feel like fate.

"And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat upon that house…" — Matthew 7:25

Don't confuse it with Asyndeton, Parataxis. Asyndeton deletes the connectives instead. Parataxis is the broader habit of coordination over subordination; polysyndeton is its most audible form.
