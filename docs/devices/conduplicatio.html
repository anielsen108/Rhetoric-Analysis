Repetition & Refrain

Conduplicatio

kon-doo-plih-KAH-tee-oh / ˌkɒndjuːplɪˈkɑːtioʊ

Picking up a key word from one sentence or clause and redeploying it prominently in the next, dragging the concept forward for re-examination. Unlike a pronoun, the full repetition insists the word itself — not just its referent — is what matters.

"This afternoon, this Easter afternoon, I saw a friend die. A friend I had known…"

Don't confuse it with Anadiplosis. Anadiplosis requires the repeat at the exact seam (last word → first word); conduplicatio just needs the key word to recur early in the next unit.
