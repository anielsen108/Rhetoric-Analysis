Repetition & Refrain

Epanalepsis

eh-puh-nuh-LEP-sis / ɛpənəˈlɛpsɪs

Repeating a clause's opening word or phrase at its own end, closing the loop: the sentence returns to where it began. The circular shape suggests obsession, inevitability, or a truth that contains itself.

"The king is dead, long live the king."

Don't confuse it with Anadiplosis. Anadiplosis repeats across a clause boundary (end of one, start of the next); epanalepsis circles within one unit.
