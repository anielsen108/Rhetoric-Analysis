Hendiadys

hen-DY-uh-dis / hɛnˈdaɪədɪs

Expressing one complex idea as two nouns joined by 'and' instead of a noun with modifier — sound and fury rather than furious sound. Splitting the idea in two makes both halves stand alone and equal, lending a strange, slightly ritual doubleness that resists paraphrase. Shakespeare's favorite estranging device.

"the sound and the fury" — Macbeth

Don't confuse it with. Not a mere pair of things: in hendiadys the two nouns secretly name *one* thing.
