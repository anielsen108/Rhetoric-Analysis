Epithet

EP-ih-thet / ˈɛpɪθɛt

A characterizing adjective or phrase attached to a name or thing — wine-dark sea, swift-footed Achilles. Repeated, it fuses with its noun and becomes identity; the quality stops being observed and starts being definitional. Homeric formula, tabloid nickname, and brand slogan share this engine.

"rosy-fingered Dawn" — Homer

Don't confuse it with. A transferred epithet (hypallage) moves the adjective to the wrong noun: 'a sleepless night' — the night wasn't sleepless; you were.
