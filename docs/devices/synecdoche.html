Synecdoche

sih-NEK-duh-kee / sɪˈnɛkdəkiː

Letting a part stand for the whole (hired hands for workers) or the whole for a part (the law for a police officer). The chosen part does silent argumentative work: calling sailors 'hands' reduces them to their labor; calling a car 'wheels' reduces it to motion.

"All hands on deck."

Don't confuse it with Metonymy. A subtype of metonymy: synecdoche requires the part/whole relation specifically, not just any association.
