Solecism

SOL-uh-siz-um / ˈsɒləsɪzəm

A deliberate breach of grammar or usage deployed for effect — dialect, double negatives, non-standard agreement. In fiction it is voice: the 'error' authenticates a speaker the standard language would erase. What the schoolroom marks wrong, the novelist banks as ethos.

"You don't know about me without you have read a book…" — Huck Finn

Don't confuse it with. An accidental solecism is just an error; the device requires the wrongness to be chosen and characterizing. Enallage is the classical name for expressive grammatical substitution.
