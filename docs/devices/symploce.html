Symploce

SIM-ploh-see / ˈsɪmploʊsiː

Anaphora and epistrophe combined: successive clauses share both their opening and their closing words, with variation trapped in the middle. The frame stays fixed while the center changes, which makes the changing element feel like the only thing in the world that moves.

"When there is talk of hatred, let us stand up and talk against it. When there is talk of violence, let us stand up and talk against it." — Bill Clinton

Don't confuse it with Parallelism. Not mere parallelism: symploce requires the identical words at both edges, not just matching grammar.
