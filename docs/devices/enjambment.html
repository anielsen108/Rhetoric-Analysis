Omission & Flow

Enjambment

en-JAM-ment / ɪnˈdʒæmmənt

A verse line that ends before its sentence does, forcing the reader over the line-break to complete the thought. The break creates a split-second of double meaning — the line means one thing alone, another once continued — and the carried-over word lands with inherited momentum.

"April is the cruellest month, breeding / Lilacs out of the dead land…" — Eliot

Don't confuse it with. An end-stopped line completes its grammar at the break. Caesura is a pause *inside* the line, not at its edge.
