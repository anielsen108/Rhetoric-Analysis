Metaphor & Figuration

Simile

SIM-ih-lee / ˈsɪməliː

An explicit comparison marked by like or as. The marker keeps both terms visible and honest — the reader is invited to test the resemblance rather than swallow an identity. Similes can afford to be more outrageous than metaphors precisely because the grammar admits they are figures.

"My love is like a red, red rose." — Burns

Don't confuse it with Metaphor. Drop the 'like' and it becomes metaphor. An epic simile extends the comparison over many lines with its own internal scene.
