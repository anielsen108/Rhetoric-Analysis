Aphorism

AF-or-iz-um / ˈæfərɪzəm

A compact, freestanding statement of general truth, polished until it feels inevitable. Aphorism converts opinion into apparent law: the balance and brevity signal that the sentence has survived long testing, even when freshly minted. Openings that begin aphoristically borrow the authority of proverbs.

"It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife." — Austen (ironic aphorism)

Don't confuse it with Irony. A maxim instructs conduct; an epigram jokes; the aphorism claims truth. Austen's is an aphorism wearing irony.
