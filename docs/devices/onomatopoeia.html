Sound & Diction

Onomatopoeia

on-uh-mat-uh-PEE-uh / ˌɒnəˌmætəˈpiːə

Words whose sound imitates their sense — buzz, crack, murmur. At full power it extends past single words: whole lines can be engineered so their phonetics perform the event they describe, making the reader's mouth enact the meaning.

"The moan of doves in immemorial elms, / And murmuring of innumerable bees" — Tennyson

Don't confuse it with Alliteration, Assonance. Alliteration and assonance pattern sounds without imitating anything; onomatopoeia requires the sound-sense match.
