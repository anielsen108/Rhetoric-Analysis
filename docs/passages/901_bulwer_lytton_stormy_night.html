The Gallery of Errors · Specimen 901

A Dark and Stormy Night

Edward Bulwer-Lytton · Paul Clifford · Opening sentence

hover a marked phrase · click to pin · chips toggle layers
1It was a dark and stormy night; the rain fell in torrents—except at occasional
2intervals, when it was checked by a violent gust of wind which swept up the
3streets (for it is in London that our scene lies), rattling along the housetops,
4and fiercely agitating the scanty flame of the lamps that struggled against the
5darkness.

Thesis of failureEvery image is issued and immediately recalled. The sentence hedges, digresses, and decorates until the storm it announces has nothing left to do.

OccasionA novel's threshold, squandered. The most infamous opening in English fiction, and the patron sentence of purple prose.
PersonaA narrator determined to be atmospheric and unwilling to stop qualifying long enough to manage it.

Full dossier

1The Autopsy

One sentence, five lines, and not one clause allowed to finish its work. The order of failure: first the hedge (torrents, retracted), then the digression (London, announced), then the decoration (lamps, ensouled). Three separate faults, each more self-indulgent than the last. The grammar is sound throughout — that is the lesson. Nothing here is a mistake of syntax. Every fault is a decision.

2The Repair

Strip the exceptions, remove the parenthesis, and let one image stand: "The night was dark and stormy. Rain swept the London streets in gusts, and the lamps guttered." Two sentences, one commitment each. The reader now knows the city, the weather, and — from what is no longer there — that the author trusts them.
