The Bistro Menu

A Neighbourhood Bistro · Autumn Menu (a composed specimen) · Starters page

hover a marked phrase · click to pin · chips toggle layers
1Prepare for a life-changing explosion of hand-crafted, artisanal flavour that
2will transport your taste buds on an unforgettable odyssey.
3Our legendary signature fries are quite literally the best thing you will ever
4eat in your entire life.

Thesis of failureHyperbole applied uniformly until it cancels itself, "literally" deployed to mean its own opposite, and epithets stacked where evidence should be. Inflation economics, performed in prose: when everything is legendary, the currency is worthless.

OccasionThe fries must justify fourteen dollars. The menu writer has elected to do this with superlatives rather than potatoes.
PersonaBreathless retail maximalism; every dish a destiny.

Full dossier

1The Autopsy

Every device here is aimed at the same target — magnitude — and that is the tell. Real praise varies its instruments: a texture, a provenance, a comparison, a fact. Uniform superlatives are the signature of a writer who has not tasted the dish, or worse, has, and is compensating. Note also the temporal madness of the final claim: "the best thing you will ever eat" mortgages the customer's entire future against a side order. No sentence should make promises its potatoes must keep.

2The Repair

Underclaim and specify. "Fries: cut this morning, fried twice in beef fat, salted once. Ask for the aioli." Not one adjective of praise — and the reader trusts every word, because the sentence spends its authority on facts and lets the eater supply the superlatives. Understatement is the only flattery a menu can afford.
