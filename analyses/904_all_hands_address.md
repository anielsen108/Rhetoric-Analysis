# Passage 904: The All-Hands Address

**A Chief Executive** · *Q3 All-Hands Transcript* (a composed specimen) · 2025 | Opening remarks

**Occasion/Pressure:** The quarter was mediocre and the executive must generate urgency without admitting anything specific went wrong.
**Persona/Stance:** Motivational management; certainty of tone in inverse proportion to precision of content.
**Thesis of Effect:** A pile-up of dead metaphors, reanimated and set loose in the same sentences, where they collide. Each figure was once alive; stacked, they assemble into pictures no one could act on.

---

## PASSAGE

[L1] Team, we need to grab the low-hanging fruit before the window of opportunity
[L2] closes on this moving target.
[L3] We are going to hit the ground running, drill down into our north star, and
[L4] move the needle until the rubber meets the road.
[L5] At the end of the day, there is no I in team, and we must all row in the same
[L6] direction, or that ship has already sailed.

---

## A) RHETORICAL TROPES USED

### **Catachresis** (the collision)
**Lines:** L1–2
**Definition:** Figures mixed past the possibility of a picture.
**Evidence:** "grab the low-hanging fruit before the window of opportunity closes on this moving target"
**Effect:** An orchard, a window, and a firing range occupy the same clause. The fruit hangs, the window closes, the target moves — three metaphors, three incompatible physics. The listener's imagination attempts all three, fails, and files the sentence under *noise*.
**Near-miss:** Shakespeare's "take arms against a sea" mixes two frames to dramatize an impossible choice. This mixes three to avoid making one.

### **Metaphor** (dead, reanimated)
**Lines:** L3–4
**Definition:** Transfer of meaning between domains — here, transfers long since expired.
**Evidence:** "drill down into our north star," "move the needle until the rubber meets the road"
**Effect:** Each phrase died honourably in some earlier decade; the trouble begins when corpses are made to interact. One cannot drill into a star. A needle that moves until rubber meets road belongs to no instrument ever built. Dead metaphors are safe only when kept apart, like retired chemicals.

### **Paradox** (accidental)
**Lines:** L5–6
**Definition:** Self-contradiction — in this case, unnoticed by its author.
**Evidence:** "we must all row in the same direction, or that ship has already sailed"
**Effect:** The crew is commanded to row a ship which, in the same breath, has left without them. Intended paradox compresses a truth; accidental paradox decompresses the speaker's authority, audibly.

---

## B) RHETORICAL SCHEMES USED

### **Enumeratio** (a tricolon of clichés)
**Lines:** L3–4
**Definition:** Items marshalled in series.
**Evidence:** "hit the ground running, drill down into our north star, and move the needle"
**Effect:** The classical tricolon promises escalation — veni, vidi, vici. This one runs, drills, and nudges: three stock phrases in a trench coat, ascending nowhere. The form raises the audience's ear for a climax and delivers an idiom inventory.

### **Aphorism** (counterfeit)
**Lines:** L5
**Definition:** A compressed general truth — or the sound of one.
**Evidence:** "there is no I in team"
**Effect:** The cadence of wisdom with the content of a spelling observation. Genuine aphorism survives paraphrase; this one, paraphrased, is "the word 'team' contains no letter I," which is true of 'committee' twice over and improves nothing.

---

## C) MODERN SYNTACTICAL APPROACHES USED

### **Filler Frames**
**Lines:** L5
**Definition:** Ritual phrases that occupy the sentence's emphatic positions while asserting nothing.
**Evidence:** "At the end of the day"
**Effect:** The sentence's opening — its position of command — is spent on a phrase meaning approximately "and now I will speak." Rhetoric is the art of spending emphasis; filler frames are emphasis embezzled.

---

## 1) THE AUTOPSY

No single phrase here is a crime; every one of them was a serviceable metaphor once, and several still function alone in casual speech. The failure is combinatorial. Metaphor asks the listener to build a picture; this passage commissions six pictures on one canvas and blames the audience when nothing coheres. And note what the fog is *for*: not one sentence names a product, a number, a decision, or a date. The mixture is not carelessness. It is camouflage.

## 2) THE REPAIR

Say the thing. *"We have three easy wins available this quarter: renew the two lapsed accounts and ship the export feature. If we do that by October, we hit our number."* One metaphor ("wins"), kept; everything else converted back into nouns a person could act on before lunch.
