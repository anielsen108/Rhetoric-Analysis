# Passage 905: The Grant Proposal

**A Municipal Subcommittee** · *Application for the Advancement of Facilitation* (a composed specimen) · 2025 | Executive summary

**Occasion/Pressure:** Public money must be requested without anyone becoming responsible for anything. The grammar has been arranged accordingly.
**Persona/Stance:** The institutional passive: a voice from nowhere, promising processes.
**Thesis of Effect:** Agentless passives, nominalization stacked on nominalization, and periphrasis as policy. Every verb that could carry responsibility has been converted into a noun and buried; what remains is syntax with no one inside it.

---

## PASSAGE

[L1] It has been determined that the implementation of the utilization of
[L2] community-based resources is a critical priority going forward.
[L3] The facilitation of the optimization of stakeholder engagement outcomes
[L4] will be operationalized in a timely manner by means of the leveraging of
[L5] synergies.

---

## A) RHETORICAL TROPES USED

### **Periphrasis** (as policy)
**Lines:** L4–5
**Definition:** The roundabout phrase where a direct one exists.
**Evidence:** "in a timely manner by means of the leveraging of synergies"
**Effect:** "In a timely manner" means "soon"; "by means of the leveraging of synergies" means "together," at five times the length. In literature, periphrasis delays for effect. Here it delays for cover — every extra word puts distance between the promise and the person who made it.

---

## B) RHETORICAL SCHEMES USED

### **Passive Voice** (agentless)
**Lines:** L1
**Definition:** The actor removed from the action.
**Evidence:** "It has been determined"
**Effect:** Determined by whom? The sentence has arranged for that question to have no answer. The agentless passive holds institutional prose together: things are determined, operationalized, and regretted, and no one is ever named.
**Near-miss:** Jefferson's "are endowed by their Creator" is passive with the agent gloriously named. This passive hides its actor on purpose.

### **Pleonasm** (the redundant future)
**Lines:** L2
**Definition:** Words surplus to the sense.
**Evidence:** "a critical priority going forward"
**Effect:** Priorities already concern the future; "going forward" adds only the sound of momentum to a sentence containing none. And "critical priority" admits that some priorities aren't.

---

## C) MODERN SYNTACTICAL APPROACHES USED

### **Nominalization Cascade**
**Lines:** L1–3
**Definition:** Verbs converted to abstract nouns, then chained with "of."
**Evidence:** "the implementation of the utilization of community-based resources," "the facilitation of the optimization of stakeholder engagement outcomes"
**Effect:** Somewhere under "the implementation of the utilization" a person *uses* something — but the verb has been buried twice. Each nominalization removes an actor and a tense; chain three and no one is left in the sentence. Nothing in it can be done, because nothing in it is a verb.

### **Empty Modification**
**Lines:** L3
**Definition:** Modifier stacks in which no word narrows any other.
**Evidence:** "stakeholder engagement outcomes"
**Effect:** Three nouns queued in a compound where each promises specificity and delivers none. Which stakeholders? Engaged how? What outcome? The meaning is always promised by the next noun, and never arrives.

---

## 1) THE AUTOPSY

Diagram one sentence and the scheme shows itself. Subject: "the facilitation." Verb: "will be operationalized." A nothing will be done to an abstraction. Prose can survive the occasional passive and the occasional nominalization — English needs both. What it cannot survive is their systematic use as *architecture*, because this architecture has a purpose: a sentence with no agent can contain no failure, and a document written entirely in such sentences is not a proposal; it is advance cover.

## 2) THE REPAIR

Reinstate the actors and their verbs. *"The library will run the programme, using the town's existing meeting rooms. We will start in March and report attendance in June."* Every verb has an owner; every promise has a date. This plan can now fail — which is exactly what makes it possible to trust.
