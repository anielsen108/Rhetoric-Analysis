# Passage 906: The Commencement Speech

**A Visiting Dignitary** · *Remarks to the Class of 2026* (a composed specimen) · 2026 | Peroration

**Occasion/Pressure:** Four hundred graduates, ninety seconds of peroration, and nothing in particular to say. The devices of oratory are all present, running on empty.
**Persona/Stance:** Ceremonial uplift; the cadence of King borrowed, the content of a greeting card installed.
**Thesis of Effect:** Anaphora repeated without escalation becomes a metronome; parallelism collapses mid-series; the imagery descends politely from destiny to doors. The form of a great speech, inflated to scale, with the argument left out.

---

## PASSAGE

[L1] Today is a day about dreams. Today is a day about destiny. Today is a day
[L2] about the doors that are opening, the roads that lie ahead, and believing in
[L3] the power of your own unique journey.
[L4] Follow your passion, follow your heart, and never stop chasing the person
[L5] you were always meant to become.

---

## A) RHETORICAL TROPES USED

### **Metaphor** (exhausted stock)
**Lines:** L2–3
**Definition:** Transfer between domains — here drawn entirely from the retired list.
**Evidence:** "doors that are opening," "roads that lie ahead," "your own unique journey"
**Effect:** Doors, roads, journeys: the three metaphors every graduate has heard since infancy, presented as revelation. A dead metaphor can still serve inside fresh syntax; three dead metaphors in series serve only to prove the speaker packed nothing of their own.

### **Bathos** (the descending series)
**Lines:** L1–2
**Definition:** A sequence that sinks where it means to climb.
**Evidence:** "dreams...destiny...the doors that are opening"
**Effect:** Dreams, then destiny — then doors. The series promises ascent and delivers carpentry. Gradatio's ladder, walked downward with ceremony. An audience cannot always say why applause feels obligatory; the diminishing series is usually why.

---

## B) RHETORICAL SCHEMES USED

### **Anaphora** (numbed)
**Lines:** L1–2
**Definition:** Successive openings on the same phrase.
**Evidence:** "Today is a day about" ×3
**Effect:** King's anaphora escalates: each "I have a dream" lifts a more concrete and costly vision. This anaphora repeats at level altitude — dreams, destiny, doors, each as weightless as the last — and repetition without escalation is not a drumbeat but a metronome. The audience does not rise; it synchronizes.
**Near-miss:** The device is identical to the masterpiece's. The cargo is the entire difference.

### **Parallelism** (collapsed)
**Lines:** L2–3
**Definition:** Matched grammatical frames for matched ideas.
**Evidence:** "the doors that are opening, the roads that lie ahead, and believing in the power"
**Effect:** Two noun-plus-relative-clause units march in step — and then a gerund falls into the ranks. "Believing" answers to nothing before it; the series breaks its own contract at the moment of climax. A collapsed parallel is worse than none: the ear, promised symmetry, audits the failure.

### **Epizeuxis-adjacent Repetition** (follow, follow)
**Lines:** L4
**Definition:** Immediate repetition for intensity.
**Evidence:** "Follow your passion, follow your heart"
**Effect:** Passion and heart are the same organ in this sentence; the repetition doubles the verb without doubling the thought. Intensity requires that the second strike land somewhere new. This lands on the same pillow.

---

## C) MODERN SYNTACTICAL APPROACHES USED

### **Vagueness at the Point of Maximum Emphasis**
**Lines:** L4–5
**Definition:** The sentence's terminal position — its natural stress — spent on an unfalsifiable abstraction.
**Evidence:** "the person you were always meant to become"
**Effect:** End-weight is the orator's premium real estate; strong speeches spend it on the concrete and the costly. This speech spends it on a person who cannot be identified, pursued by a chase that cannot fail or succeed. The grammar peaks precisely where the meaning is thinnest.

---

## 1) THE AUTOPSY

Set this beside King and the diagnosis writes itself. The machinery is identical — anaphora, series, terminal climax. What is missing is *risk*: King's repetitions each raised the price of the dream (Georgia, Mississippi, his own children); these repetitions raise nothing, ask nothing, and could be delivered unchanged at any school in any decade. The forms of oratory are amplifiers. Amplify a conviction and you get a movement. Amplify a vacancy and you get — today, a day, about dreams.

## 2) THE REPAIR

Keep the anaphora; give it cargo that escalates. *"Today you owe your parents a phone call. Today you owe your rivals a handshake. And by this day next year, you owe one stranger a debt they cannot repay — go and incur it."* The frame repeats; the price rises each time; the final position holds an instruction someone could actually disobey.
